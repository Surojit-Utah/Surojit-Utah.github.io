Disentanglement Analysis in Deep Latent Variable Models Matching Aggregate Posterior Distributions

Surojit Saha, Sarang Joshi, Ross Whitaker · Scientific Computing and Imaging Institute, University of Utah
IEEE International Conference on Acoustics, Speech and Signal Processing (ICASSP), 2025
Paper PDF arXiv Code

In short: standard disentanglement metrics assume each generative factor lines up with one latent coordinate axis. That holds for VAEs, but not for models that match their aggregate posterior to an isotropic Gaussian, whose latent space can be freely rotated. Evaluating along PCA-discovered directions instead of the coordinate axes gives these models a fair score.

The problem

FactorVAE, MIG and DCI scores measure whether each ground-truth factor is captured by a single latent coordinate. VAEs encourage this through their factorized Gaussian posterior. But a family of models, including the AVAE, adversarial autoencoders, Wasserstein autoencoders and GENs, matches the aggregate posterior to an isotropic Gaussian. Rotating their latent space changes nothing about reconstruction or the latent distribution, so their factors may lie along arbitrary directions, and standard metrics wrongly score them as entangled.

Approach

  • Discover directions, don't assume them. Fix one ground-truth factor, vary the others, and apply PCA to the encoded samples. The direction of least variance corresponds to the fixed factor.
  • Aggregate the estimates. Repeat across many batches and combine the estimates by eigendecomposition of their mean outer product, giving a stable set of latent directions.
  • Re-score along those directions. PCA FactorVAE and PCA MIG apply the standard metrics to codes projected onto the discovered directions. The framework works for any deep latent variable model.

Results

Mean over 10 runs. Aggregate-posterior models gain substantially, while axis-aligned VAEs change little, which shows the new metrics correct a bias rather than inflating every score.
